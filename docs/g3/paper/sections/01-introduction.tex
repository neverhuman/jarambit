\section{Introduction}
A karambit is a curved, ring-handled knife held in a closed fist with the little finger through the ring. Printing one
in Ti-6Al-4V by laser powder-bed fusion makes a hollow, lattice-filled part possible, which opens a large design space
but also makes it easy to fool oneself: a coarse voxel model can hide mass, a strength formula can credit material that
is never built, and an edge can be left unground where a test does not look. G3 is the generation of the Jarambit
project that designs the whole knife around the hand. This paper reports its v\gVersion{} release.

Three choices define the release. First, \emph{evolution designs the knife}: populations start from scrambled Sobol
samples, nobody edits an individual, and engineering requirements enter as constraints on what the decoder can express
or as measured terms of the objective. Second, \emph{the edge is part of the design space, not a post-process}: both
cutting edges must carry one primary grind at a knife-appropriate angle, run unbroken to the point, and be serrated
with teeth that do not snap (Section~\ref{sec:design}). Third, \emph{only measurements are reported}: every number in
this paper and in the repository README is generated from one release receipt, and the strength figures quoted for
the design come from voxel-SDF geometry, plate and solid finite elements, and a voxel finite-element certification of
the printed geometry.

The contributions are the decoder rules for edges and serrations (EDGE-1..5, SERR-1..4), the logged evolution of a
\gIslands-island cohort, the design it found, and a measurement chain whose every step is reproducible from a commit
and a genome row (Section~\ref{sec:repro}).
