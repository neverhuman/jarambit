\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
A karambit is a curved knife with a finger ring at the pommel, held in a closed fist; in this design the little finger
goes through the ring. Printing one in Ti-6Al-4V by laser powder-bed fusion (L-PBF) makes a hollow part possible: the
grip and the blade core become a skin over a triply periodic minimal-surface (TPMS) lattice with ports to remove the
powder. That opens a large three-dimensional design space, and with it several ways for a search to fool itself. A
coarse voxel grid can hide mass. A beam or plate formula can credit material that is never built. A blade can keep an
unground shoulder where a sparse sharpness check does not look. And deep, narrow serrations can lengthen the edge while
leaving needle teeth that snap on the first hard snag.

G3 is the generation of the Jarambit project that designs the whole knife around the hand: the ring, the grip, the
guard, the hollow interior and a double-edged, double-serrated claw blade come from one decoder and are scored by one
multi-fidelity evaluation chain. This paper reports its v\gVersion{} release and the best individual of the cohort of
record, \gDesign{} (decoder commit \gCommit, decoder hash \gDecoder).

Three choices define the release.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Evolution designs the knife.} Every island starts from scrambled Sobol samples
(\src{crates/g3/src/g3_genome.rs}{\code{g3_genome.rs}}); no seed design, template or hand edit enters a population.
Engineering requirements enter either as constraints on what the decoder can express or as terms of the objective.
\item \emph{The edge is part of the design space, not a post-process.} The decoder gives both cutting edges one primary
grind at 17.5--22.5\textdegree{} per side, measured normal to the actual serrated edge and run unbroken to the point
(EDGE-1..5), and serrates both edges within bounds chosen so that the teeth do not snap (SERR-1..4;
Section~\ref{sec:design}). The strength of every tooth between two scallops is checked under a \gToothSnag{} N snag and a
\gToothPry{} N pry, on 60 Sobol designs in the decoder's tests and on the released design
(\src{crates/g3/src/g3_qd.rs}{\code{serration_tooth_strength}}).
\item \emph{Every computed number has one source.} Each computed quantity of the design in this paper and in the
repository README is generated from one release receipt, \code{evidence/g3/v\gRecord/release.json}, by
\src{crates/g3/src/bin/g3_release_tex.rs}{\code{g3-release-tex}} and \src{ops/ci/gen_readme.py}{\code{gen_readme.py}}; the
rule constants (gene bounds, loads, allowables) are the decoder's own and are quoted as written. The measurements are
voxel geometry, plate and solid finite elements and a voxel finite-element check of the printed part; three
quantities are not finite-element results and are labelled where they appear: the buckling factor (an analytic
estimate), the tooth safety factors (a beam formula on the exact section law) and the edge geometry (measured on the
exact section law rather than on voxels).
\end{enumerate}
