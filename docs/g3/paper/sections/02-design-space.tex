\section{Design space}\label{sec:design}
\subsection{Genome and decoder}
A G3.0 genome is a vector of 179 genes $\mathbf{u} \in [0,1]^{179}$
(\src{crates/g3/src/g3_genome.rs}{\code{g3_genome.rs}}). \code{G3Genome::decode} maps it to named physical parameters,
from which the decoder builds a signed distance field (SDF) of the whole knife
(\src{crates/g3/src/g3_sdf.rs}{\code{g3_sdf.rs}}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ring and grip.} The finger ring has a fixed 24.9~mm bore and an 8~mm lip width (gene \code{ring_lip_w} locked to 8.0~mm in r2); the lip's radial
depth is a gene (1.5--2.8~mm). A filleted junction joins it to a compact grip with a palm swell, a variable section and
three finger grooves (ring, middle and index finger)\cite{pheasant2006}. The grip surface carries a procedural texture blended from five
bases: knurl, a sinusoidal stripe pattern, Voronoi cells, longitudinal ridges and a small compositional
pattern-producing network.
\item \textbf{Guard and blade.} An integral guard leads to a curved claw blade whose straight-line reach from the guard
is capped at 85.725~mm (3.375~in, \code{BLADE_REACH_MAX_MM}) in the chord solver (after serration-scallop and edge-curve offsets, the Euclidean tip-to-guard distance can exceed this cap by up to $\sim$0.5~mm); its hook drop is a gene. The blade thickness tapers
via a smoothstep cubic Hermite curve ($3u^2 - 2u^3$) from a core value (4--7~mm) to a tip value (1.5--4.5~mm), with a dorsal ridge and fuller, and the last
6--12~mm before the point follow a five-knot monotone profile.
\item \textbf{Hollow interior.} Inside a skin of 0.8--1.2~mm, the grip, guard and blade core are filled with a blend of
Gyroid and Schwarz-D lattices (\code{tpms_val}, \code{tpms_level_for_rho}) with stiffening ribs and shear webs, and
powder-removal ports open the cavity to the outside. The hand-zone mask is cleaned by variable-radius morphological
closing and opening, and \code{enforce_am_connectivity} makes the build printable: air is flooded from the outside of
the grid, any sealed pocket is filled solid (so trapped powder is never counted as saved mass), and only the largest
connected solid is kept.
\end{itemize}
The decoder revision of this release, r2, is identified by the SHA-256 hash \gDecoder{}, computed by
\code{g3_decoder_sha256} (\src{crates/g3/src/g3_qd.rs}{\code{g3_qd.rs}}) over the decoder's source files, the shared
physics and CAD sources they call, and both lock files; an archive written under another hash fails its integrity
check.

\subsection{Edge rules (EDGE-1..5)}
Both the inner edge (edge A) and the spine edge (edge B) are cutting edges, and five rules make each a real grind rather
than a chamfer on a strut.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{EDGE-1, one full-height grind.} Each edge has a single primary grind, carried outward until it meets the
blade body, with no micro-bevel and no shoulder; at the guard-to-blade seam the grind takes precedence over the
hand-zone rounding, so the plunge keeps its thin bevel.
\item \textbf{EDGE-2, normal to the real edge.} The bevel depth is the exact distance $d$ to the actual, serrated edge
polyline, so the angle is the same around the curved claw and inside every scallop.
\item \textbf{EDGE-3, the angle.} The per-side angle $\theta$ is a gene bounded to 17.5--22.5\textdegree{}
(35--45\textdegree{} inclusive, centred on 20\textdegree, a hard-use edge for titanium), and the apex flat
$t_\mathrm{apex}$ is 0.05--0.12~mm. The grind thickness is
\begin{equation}
t(d) = t_\mathrm{apex} + 2\,d \tan\theta\,\bigl(1 + 0.18\,c\,\min(1, (d - 0.4)^+/6)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where the grind-curvature gene $c \in [-0.35, 0.45]$ makes the grind slightly hollow or convex beyond the first
0.4~mm, by $-6$ to $+8\,\%$ in slope. This is why the bevel angles in Table~\ref{tab:best} span a little more than the
apex angles.
\item \textbf{EDGE-4, to the point.} Both grinds run unbroken to the point. Only the plunge by the guard is
unsharpened: the grind begins 5--12\,\% of the way along the edge (a gene) and ramps in over 3~mm. Each sharpened edge
is capped at 101.6~mm (4.0~in).
\item \textbf{EDGE-5, the check.} The sharpness gate (\code{measure_blade_edge_apex_and_angle}) samples the exact
section law along the inward edge normal at 0, 0.4 and 2.0~mm behind the apex, at every station whose grind is steady,
on both edges. The worst station sets the sharpness terms, so a blunt station or a shoulder behind the apex costs score.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Serration rules (SERR-1..4)}
Both edges are serrated, under four rules.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{SERR-1, depth.} The serration depth is 0.8--2.2~mm on the inner edge and 0.8--2.0~mm on the spine edge,
so no design has a plain edge.
\item \textbf{SERR-2, count.} The pitch is capped on the part of the span cut to full depth, so every serrated span
holds at least three full-depth scallops.
\item \textbf{SERR-3, placement.} The span starts 16--24\,\% of the way along the blade, past the plunge, and ends at
least $\max(\text{tip reserve} + 3, 18)$~mm before the point, so the piercing point stays plain.
\item \textbf{SERR-4, teeth that do not snap.} The pitch is 5--9~mm and the depth at most a quarter of the pitch.
\end{enumerate}
These bounds are what the search sees. The strength of the resulting teeth is checked separately
(\src{crates/g3/src/g3_qd.rs}{\code{serration_tooth_strength}}), on 60 Sobol designs in the decoder's tests and on the
released design; it is not a term of the score. Every tooth between two neighbouring scallops is checked on the exact
2D section law. Its root is the segment of length $b$ between the two gullet bottoms, and its height $h_t$ is the
outline's furthest point from that segment. Sampling the blade thickness $t(s)$ at 40 points along the root gives the
in-plane second moment $I_\mathrm{ip}$ about the root's thickness-weighted centroid, the extreme-fibre distance $c$ from
that centroid, the out-of-plane second moment $I_\mathrm{op} = \int t^3/12\,\mathrm{d}s$ and the largest thickness
$t_\mathrm{max}$. Two load cases act at the tooth tip, a snag of $F_\mathrm{s} = \gToothSnag$~N along the edge and a pry of
$F_\mathrm{p} = \gToothPry$~N across the blade:
\begin{gather}
\sigma_\mathrm{snag} = K_t\,\frac{F_\mathrm{s}\, h_t\, c}{I_\mathrm{ip}}, \qquad K_t = 1 + 2\sqrt{h_t/\rho}, \\
\sigma_\mathrm{pry} = \frac{F_\mathrm{p}\, h_t\, t_\mathrm{max}/2}{I_\mathrm{op}},
\end{gather}
where $\rho$ is the smaller radius of the circles through each gullet bottom and the outline $\pm0.6$~mm either side
of it (the Inglis notch factor~\cite{inglis1913,peterson1974}, applied to the in-plane snag term). The tooth's safety factor is
$755~\mathrm{MPa}/\max(\sigma_\mathrm{snag}, \sigma_\mathrm{pry})$ (taking the larger of the two decoupled load cases rather than their von Mises combination), using the Ti-6Al-4V yield stress of 828~MPa~\cite{astmf3001} derated
by 0.912. Because each interior tooth is defined between two adjacent gullet minima, $N$ scallops define $N-1$ interior teeth; the decoder's tests require at least 1 for every checked interior tooth of the 60 designs.
