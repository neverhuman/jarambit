\noindent\textbf{Abstract---}Computational design can search geometric families that are impractical to enumerate manually, but a persuasive shape and a rising fitness curve are insufficient engineering evidence. This white paper studies Jarambit, a Rust-centered evolutionary CAD platform that connects a parameterized representation, numerical assessment, weighted ranking, and retained evidence. We explain the lifecycle of a gene, the distinction between searching a fixed genome and proposing a new representation, and the role agents could play in the latter. A complete assessment taxonomy separates 16 legacy diagnostic predicates, ten overlapping requirement groups, 18 heterogeneous battery evaluators, and 178 frozen scientific-assurance definitions. Reconciliation of the historical battery yields 414 retained cells: \BatteryPass\ stored passes, \BatteryFail\ failures, and \BatteryBlocked\ blocked records. These labels include capability and completeness records; they are not counts of independent physical experiments. A selected retained weighted cohort reports best fitness \BestFitness\ on its higher-is-better scale, with no qualified design. Its three-generation history cannot establish long-run convergence or compute scaling. A separate historical dataset contains \BirthCount\ evaluated births and supports population analysis within its original score regime. We distinguish implemented methods, retrospective findings, and prospective agent-assisted research. The resulting framework connects ambitious design exploration to numerical verification, source identity, honest reporting, and the physical measurement needed before additive-manufactured products can be trusted.
\par\vspace{3mm}
