\noindent\textbf{Abstract---}Executable representations allow evolutionary search to explore geometric families while preserving a concrete path from genes to CAD and numerical observations. This paper studies Jarambit, a Rust-centered platform linking a parameterized representation, evolutionary search, and multiple assessment stages. A retrospective population contains \BirthCount\ retained births over 17 generations: \FemComplete\ records report completed FEM, while \FemNotEvaluated\ were not evaluated by FEM. Their stored fitness combines legality-dependent penalties with contextual novelty, so population-score changes cannot be read directly as mechanical improvement. The highest stored search score, \HistoricalBestFit, belongs to an individual that did not pass the later strict assessment. The historical producer reports that search passers did not beat its baseline; separately constructed controlled probes supplied the named exported examples. A later weighted reporting cohort provides short summaries whose stage lineage is incomplete and whose values are not comparable with the historical score. We explain the implemented representation and assessment hierarchy, reconcile the retained observations, and distinguish numerical verification from physical validation. The resulting case study shows why search progress, assessment outcomes, and artifact identity must be analyzed together. Agent-assisted representation design and additive manufacturing provide an expansive research direction, while claims of agent superiority, product qualification, and achieved manufacturing precision remain open.
