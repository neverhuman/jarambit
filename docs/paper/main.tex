\noindent\textbf{Abstract---}Engineered Intelligence treats physical design as an executable process: a genome defines geometry, numerical models evaluate it, and an automated population search explores alternatives. We present Jarambit, a Rust-centered research system connecting a 128-coordinate geometric genome and categorical material state to CAD decoding, island-based evolutionary search, and multiple finite-element assessment models. Its retained records include \CohortThreeTotalEvaluations\ evaluations across \CohortThreeRuns\ four-island runs spanning generations $0$--$800$ (\text{Run~2}--\text{Run~11}, \CohortThreeRowsPerRun\ births per run, \CohortThreeLegalEvaluations\ search-legal), with complete compact genome and score tables, alongside a reconstructable historical record (\text{Run~1}, \CohortOneBirths\ births across generations $0$--$16$) that exposes parentage, $2{,}176$ island-slot memberships, $192$ elite reappearances, and $12$ inter-island migrations, plus \CohortTwoBirthEvents\ intermediate multi-stage screening proposals (\CohortTwoScreenRows\ joined to Stage-1 observations and \CohortTwoUnscreenedBirths\ unscreened). Source-bound population views show the complete retained search record while keeping different scorers and assessment stages separate. The evidence also exposes a critical limitation: a high search score does not establish later mechanical qualification; the retained historical top-ranked birth (Individual~\CohortOneBestId, $f=\CohortOneBestFit$) and the single assessed automatic-pipeline export (Individual~2000; 1 assessed candidate, 0 qualified) fail strict verification. Matched-mesh quadratic solid solver agreement ($<0.05\%$) and refused plate grid-convergence estimates provide complementary checks rather than proof of physical performance. The contribution is an inspectable connection between representation, search, and engineering evidence.
