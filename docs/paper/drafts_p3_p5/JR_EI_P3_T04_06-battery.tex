\section{Numerical Observation Families and Methodological Evolution}
\label{sec:battery}
\IEEEPARstart{E}{valuating} an evolved physical geometry requires translating continuous shape variations into concrete geometric and mechanical observables. In earlier drafts of the Jarambit documentation, forty-four catalog identifiers---sixteen legacy \code{G16} diagnostics, ten grouped \code{RS-v2} requirement predicates, and eighteen historical \code{battery} columns---were listed inline in the main manuscript alongside a 414-cell categorical status matrix. While complete, that administrative structure allowed test-catalog taxonomy to overshadow the physical and numerical methods.

In this section, we reorganize the main-text presentation around the \textbf{eleven substantive observation and model families} defined by the underlying physics and software architecture (Table~\ref{tab:observation-families}). Every subsection follows a uniform seven-part structure: (1)~engineering question, (2)~represented observable and units, (3)~numerical domain and formulation, (4)~role across historical and weighted objective regimes, (5)~retained visual evidence for the assessed export Individual~2000, (6)~interpretation limits, and (7)~exact links to the 44-entry Test Atlas and Supplement~S1--S3. The complete 23-subject by 18-evaluator historical battery matrix (\BatteryPass\ stored passes, \BatteryFail\ failures, and \BatteryBlocked\ blocked cells; \code{fresh\_this\_lane: false}) and the item-by-item 44-entry crosswalk are preserved without omission in the Supplement.

\subsection{Gate and Objective Evolution Across Regimes}
\label{subsec:gate-evolution}
Understanding the retained data requires distinguishing three evaluation regimes that coexist in the repository without conflating their semantics:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Historical Native Search (Cohort~1 \text{Run~1}, Cohort~3 \text{Run~2}--\text{Run~11}, and \code{crescent} QD):} During native quality-diversity evolution (\src{crates/crescent/src/qd.rs}{\code{qd.rs}}), each birth is decoded and checked sequentially for geometric legality, reference-shape conformity (\code{aes\_pass}), and plate finite-element feasibility. The historical scalar fitness $f$ is therefore \emph{piecewise and conditional}: candidates failing geometric legality or aesthetic floor receive negative penalty branches without invoking FEM (\FemNotEvaluated\ of \BirthCount\ births in Cohort~1 \text{Run~1}), whereas evaluated candidates combine normalized structural response ratios, mass terms, and archive-contextual novelty. Removing the categorical pass/fail matrix from the main narrative does not make the historical search algorithm gate-free; its conditional branch structure is preserved as an explicit methodological fact.
  \item \textbf{Two-Stage Weighted Reporting and Automatic Orchestration (\code{auto\_evolve.rs}):} In the newer automatic pipeline (\src{crates/cli/src/auto_evolve.rs}{\code{auto\_evolve.rs}}), Stage~1 (\code{stage1\_ultralight\_weighted\_fitness}) screens candidates using fast plate and geometric observables, while Stage~2 (\code{stage2\_ultralight\_weighted\_fitness} and \code{certify\_candidate\_stage2}) adds local quadratic-solid and refined observations. The 20-term weighted reporting profile $F = \sum_{i=1}^{20} w_i s_i \in [0,100]$ aggregates four thematic groups (30\% mass/compactness, 25\% form/comfort proxies, 15\% exported geometry, 30\% response/verification). Prior to \href{https://github.com/neverhuman/jarambit/pull/135}{PR~135} (\code{72e25a360470}), Stage-2 selection and Python reporting differed on two placeholder geometry inputs, and \code{round\_ultralight\_maxima} combined stage values across rounds. We therefore keep historical native $f$, Stage-1 screen scores, and Stage-2 weighted profiles strictly separate.
  \item \textbf{Fixed Historical Assessment Battery (23 Subjects $\times$ 18 Columns):} The historical CAD battery evaluates 23 fixed subjects (21 earlier-cohort subjects and two Cohort~2 controlled-probe subjects including Individual~2001) across 18 heterogeneous columns mixing shared software controls (92 of the \BatteryPass\ passes), missing geometry-binding receipts (\BatteryBlocked\ blocked cells across \code{geom.unit}, \code{geom.one\_solid}, \code{geom.pin}, and four reference rows), record-completeness flags (\code{solid.tip}), and strict grouped gates (\code{gate.rs\_v2}, with 19 failures and 4 blocked reference rows). Its exhaustive cell-level audit is provided in Supplement~S1.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Family 1: Representation and Exported Geometry}
\label{subsec:family-geometry}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} Does a decoded genome produce a closed, single-solid B-rep and surface mesh with verifiable unit scaling, mass ($\mathrm{g}$), edge/tip profile geometry ($\mathrm{mm}$), and cryptographic byte binding?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} The decoder maps the 128-gene vector and material density into a 2D mid-surface contour, variable thickness/bevel fields, and 3D STEP/STL exports (\src{ops/ci/verify_genome_export.py}{\code{verify\_genome\_export.py}}, \src{crates/crescent/src/gates.rs}{\code{gates.rs}}, \src{crates/crescent/src/pheno.rs}{\code{pheno.rs}}).
\textbf{Objective Role:} Geometric legality gates initial evaluation in native QD search; exported geometry terms contribute 15\% of the weighted reporting profile.
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:export-identity} (\code{export-identity.svg}) binds the assessed example's genome, STEP/STL digests, 1 solid / 1 shell / 55,698 STEP face tokens, OpenCascade \code{valid=true} record, and 44,994-vertex / 90,000-triangle Blender render mesh. Three catalog entries in this family (\code{G16-02}, \code{G16-03}, and \code{RSV2-09}/\code{10}) intentionally have no generated subsection visual (`entries_missing_subsection_visual = 4` across the 44-entry catalog) because nominal parameters and exported B-rep metrology must not be conflated. In the historical battery, \code{geom.unit}, \code{geom.one\_solid}, and \code{geom.pin} are blocked across all 23 subjects (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{g16-cad\_watertight\_and\_single\_solid}, \code{g16-geometric\_and\_holder\_gates}, \code{g16-karambit\_ring\_and\_curved\_claw}, \code{rsv2-gate\_s\_sharp\_law}, \code{rsv2-gate\_s\_tip\_point\_brep}, \code{battery-geom.*}).

\subsection{Family 2: Shape and Reference Comparison}
\label{subsec:family-reference}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} How closely does the decoded exterior silhouette match the authored reference archetype in Hausdorff distance ($\mathrm{mm}$), curvature-histogram Earth Mover's Distance (EMD, dimensionless), and composite \code{aes\_score} $\in [0,1]$?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} Evaluates the closed 2D exterior polyline (4,230 points for the assessed export) against the retained C.5 exterior reference polyline (1,117 points, reused by C.6) under a common isotropic coordinate transform (\src{crates/crescent/src/aesthetic.rs}{\code{aesthetic.rs}}).
\textbf{Objective Role:} Enforces the \code{aes\_pass} floor before FEM in native QD search and supplies form/reference proxy terms in weighted reporting.
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:reference} (\code{reference.svg}) displays both polylines and the saved descriptors (Hausdorff $2.285\,\mathrm{mm}$, curvature EMD $0.0071$, \code{aes\_score} $0.4079$, empty failure list). Reference resemblance measures conformity to an authored geometric prior, not human ergonomic approval or comfort (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{g16-c6\_aesthetic\_floor}, \code{rsv2-aesthetic\_and\_geometric}).

\subsection{Family 3: Plate Lateral Response}
\label{subsec:family-lateral}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} What is the linear-elastic bending response per unit lateral load ($\mathrm{MPa/N}$) and the resulting lateral load capacity $F_{\mathrm{lat}}$ ($\mathrm{N}$) across coarse, certification, and strict plate discretizations?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} Discrete Kirchhoff Triangle (DKT) bending elements on the 2D mid-surface ($13{,}503$ nodes and $25{,}209$ triangles at the $0.25\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ level; minima taken over $0.25$ and $0.125\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ for certification, and $0.0625\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ for strict refinement in \src{crates/crescent/src/fem.rs}{\code{fem.rs}} and \src{crates/cli/src/bench.rs}{\code{bench.rs}}). Surface von Mises stress is extrapolated from Gauss points to element corners.
\textbf{Objective Role:} Primary structural capacity term in native QD fitness and the response/verification group (30\%) of the weighted profile.
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:plate-fields}B shows the $0.25\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ lateral unit-response field ($25{,}209$ valid triangles), while Fig.~\ref{fig:plate-fields}A shows the scaled multi-case envelope ($25{,}134$ valid triangles, with $75$ tip-exclusion cells masked in gray; peak $1{,}388.6695\,\mathrm{MPa}$). Figure~\ref{fig:assessment}C records the certification ($168.9008\,\mathrm{N}$) and strict ($168.1786\,\mathrm{N}$) lateral capacities. Legacy labels \code{cert\_hiker\_180n} and \code{cert\_hunter\_260n} apply two thresholds to the same lateral response family rather than running two independent experiments, and no full spatial field is archived for the $0.0625\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ strict mesh (\code{strict\_plate\_field: archive\_required}) (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{g16-cert\_hiker\_180n}, \code{g16-cert\_hunter\_260n}, \code{g16-strict\_hunter\_260n}, \code{rsv2-floors\_828\_joint}, \code{rsv2-strict\_hunter\_lateral}, \code{battery-plate.loads}).

\subsection{Family 4: Ring Traction}
\label{subsec:family-ring}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} What is the in-plane membrane von Mises stress per unit ring-pull resultant ($\mathrm{MPa/N}$) and the static ring-pull capacity ($\mathrm{N}$)?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} Six-node quadratic membrane triangles (T6) on the mid-surface under static ring-bore traction (\src{crates/crescent/src/fem.rs}{\code{fem.rs}}). In the retained $25{,}209$-triangle plate mesh, $14{,}875$ cells in the seated handle/blade region are producer-zeroed, leaving $10{,}334$ active triangles in the ring and immediate transition domain.
\textbf{Objective Role:} Contributes to the joint structural floor in native search and Stage-1/Stage-2 response scoring.
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:plate-fields}C renders the $10{,}334$ valid triangles and explicitly masks the $14{,}875$ producer-zeroed seated cells in neutral gray so excluded domain is never misread as zero stress. Static elastic ring traction is not a dynamic drop test or human-finger injury model (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{g16-cert\_ring\_pull\_1500n}, \code{rsv2-floors\_828\_joint}, \code{rsv2-human\_max}).

\subsection{Family 5: Body and Local Plate Torsion}
\label{subsec:family-torsion}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} How do the body region and terminal tip region respond to a $1.82\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ torsional load in unit response ($\mathrm{MPa/(N\,mm)}$) and regional safety factors ($\mathrm{SF}$, dimensionless)?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} DKT plate torsion model with distinct regional masks: body torsion excludes a $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ terminal tip zone ($75$ cells at $0.25\,\mathrm{mm}^2$) when computing body capacity and the global envelope, whereas local tip torsion evaluates the terminal zone at certification ($0.25/0.125\,\mathrm{mm}^2$) and strict ($0.0625\,\mathrm{mm}^2$) levels.
\textbf{Objective Role:} Separates global blade/handle torsional stiffness from localized tip fragility across both search and assessment stages.
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:plate-fields}D shows the unmasked raw torsion unit response across all $25{,}209$ triangles with the $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ capacity-exclusion boundary dashed; Fig.~\ref{fig:assessment}C pairs the certification ($0.5239$) and strict ($0.5287$) local tip safety factors. Two-dimensional Kirchhoff plate theory cannot capture three-dimensional through-thickness shear near a sharp point, motivating Family~6 (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{g16-twist\_body\_1p82nm}, \code{g16-twist\_tip\_only\_1p82nm}, \code{g16-twist\_tip\_submodel\_strict\_1p82nm}, \code{rsv2-twist\_floor\_body\_and\_tipsub\_strict}).

\subsection{Family 6: Quadratic Solid Submodel}
\label{subsec:family-solid}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} What is the three-dimensional continuum safety factor ($\mathrm{SF}_{\mathrm{solid}}$, dimensionless) in the terminal tip region under $1.82\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ torsion?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} Ten-node quadratic tetrahedral ($P_2$) continuum submodel of the terminal tip region (\src{fem_solid/tip_solid.py}{\code{tip\_solid.py}}), recording nodal-maximum and regionally binned safety factors.
\textbf{Objective Role:} Stage-2 certification gate and high-fidelity verification anchor.
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:verification}C--D plots the matched-mesh scalar safety factors and regional longitudinal profiles. No full 3D volumetric stress field array is retained in the publication inputs (\code{solved\_solid\_field: archive\_required}). Furthermore, in the historical 23-subject battery, the \code{solid.tip} column records 20 passes that signify \emph{record completeness}, not 20 favorable strength margins (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{g16-solid\_tip\_3d\_p2\_1p82nm}, \code{rsv2-solid\_tip\_sf}, \code{battery-solid.tip}).

\subsection{Family 7: Compliant Support and Linear Stability}
\label{subsec:family-support}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} Under compliant bore-bearing springs and a Winkler grip-pad foundation, what are the lateral regional stress ratio and the lowest linear buckling eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ (dimensionless)?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} Alternate DKT/CST assembly (\src{crates/physics/src/side_a4.rs}{\code{side\_a4.rs}}, \src{ops/ci/side_a4.py}{\code{side\_a4.py}}) solving static compliant support (reported ratio $5.2027$ vs.\ multi-case certification comparator $1.4353$) and linear eigenbuckling ($\lambda_1 = 7.0351$) via \code{eigsh} on $\mathbf{K}_s^{-1}\mathbf{G}$.
\textbf{Objective Role:} Side-report stability diagnostic in assessment and historical battery column \code{side.a4}.
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:assessment}A presents the support schematic and archived scalars. Because standard Hermitian Lanczos (\code{eigsh}) requires an operator self-adjoint in the inner product and symmetric $\mathbf{K}_s, \mathbf{G}$ do not make $\mathbf{K}_s^{-1}\mathbf{G}$ Euclidean-symmetric without a $\mathbf{K}_s$-inner-product formulation or retained eigenpair residual, the numerical-operator audit remains explicitly open (\code{buckling\_mode: archive\_required}) (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{g16-side\_a4\_buckling\_and\_support}, \code{battery-side.a4}).

\subsection{Family 8: Regional Yield-Order Proxy}
\label{subsec:family-sequence}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} Does the modeled elastic response ratio between designated body/ring and tip regions (\code{seq\_ratio\_cert}, dimensionless) exceed the $1.2$ ordering threshold?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} Quotient of regional linear-elastic capacity factors evaluated across the plate mesh sequence (\src{crates/crescent/src/fem.rs}{\code{fem.rs}}).
\textbf{Objective Role:} Penalizes tip-first elastic overload in native search and weighted reporting.
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:verification}A plots the sequence proxy across the three plate mesh levels. Because linear elasticity models neither plastic redistribution, crack initiation, nor dynamic fracture propagation, a favorable sequence ratio is an elastic design proxy rather than a proven physical failure sequence (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{g16-failure\_sequence\_1p2}, \code{rsv2-seq\_hard}, \code{battery-plate.seq}).

\subsection{Family 9: Fatigue Model}
\label{subsec:family-fatigue}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} What is the modified-Goodman endurance safety factor $n_f$ (dimensionless) under a repeated zero-to-maximum load cycle?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} Evaluates $n_f^{-1} = \sigma_a / S_e + \sigma_m / S_u$ with $\sigma_a = \sigma_m = \sigma_{\max}/2$ and Marin-corrected Ti-6Al-4V endurance limit $S_e$ (\src{crates/cli/src/bench.rs}{\code{bench.rs}}, \src{crates/crescent/src/fem.rs}{\code{fem.rs}}).
\textbf{Objective Role:} Stage-2 and RS-v2 durability screen (saved scalar $n_f = 0.4718$ for the assessed export; 19 unfavorable and 4 blocked in the historical battery).
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:assessment}B displays the saved scalar $0.4718$. No spatial fatigue field is stored (\code{fatigue\_field: archive\_required}), and nominal Goodman factors do not substitute for surface-roughness metrology, residual-stress measurement, or cyclic coupon testing (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{rsv2-fatigue}, \code{battery-plate.l7}).

\subsection{Family 10: Discretization and Separate-Solver Consistency}
\label{subsec:family-verification}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} Do three-level plate mesh sequences admit an asymptotic Grid Convergence Index (GCI), and do independent finite-element implementations agree on the matched $P_2$ solid mesh?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} Three-level Richardson extrapolation and refusal predicates (\src{crates/crescent/src/gci.rs}{\code{gci.rs}}, \src{fem_solid/gci.py}{\code{gci.py}}) plus matched-mesh $P_2$ tetrahedral solves comparing the native Rust solver against FEniCSx/DOLFINx (\src{fem_solid/cross_check.py}{\code{cross\_check.py}}).
\textbf{Objective Role:} Verification and credibility gates (Section~\ref{sec:verification}).
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:verification}A--B shows that while mesh-sequence metadata are valid (\code{G16-15}), oscillatory and nonpositive observed orders cause the GCI error estimates to be refused. Figure~\ref{fig:verification}C shows that native Rust and DOLFINx safety factors on the identical $P_2$ mesh agree within $0.05\%$ (\code{G16-16}), confirming solver-implementation parity on a shared discretization without bounding discretization or modeling error (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{g16-plate\_gci\_valid\_3level}, \code{g16-dolfinx\_cross\_check\_2pct}, \code{battery-plate.gci}, \code{battery-build.dolfinx}).

\subsection{Family 11: Software and Result-Completeness Controls}
\label{subsec:family-controls}
\textbf{Question \& Observables:} Do unit/invariance tests, GCI refusal arithmetic, claim-admission gates, and historical side-report references behave as specified?
\textbf{Domain \& Formulation:} Automated positive and negative software fixtures (\src{crates/physics/tests/invariance.rs}{\code{invariance.rs}}, \src{fem_solid/tests/test_gci.py}{\code{test\_gci.py}}, \src{ops/ci/check_claims.py}{\code{check\_claims.py}}) and historical battery columns \code{build.analytic}, \code{build.gci\_contracts}, \code{build.claims}, \code{build.dolfinx}, \code{side.a1..a3}, and \code{gate.rs\_v2}.
\textbf{Objective Role:} Fail-closed repository governance; software-control passes are never weighted as mechanical fitness.
\textbf{Retained Visual \& Limits:} Figure~\ref{fig:assessment}D summarizes the 10 met \code{G16} and 2 met \code{RS-v2} groups for the assessed export, while Supplement~S1 (Fig.~S1, \code{battery-matrix.svg}) retains all 414 historical cells, explaining why 92 repeated software-control passes and 57 shared \code{side.a1..a3} references must be separated from candidate-specific mechanical tests (\textbf{Atlas:} \code{battery-build.*}, \code{battery-side.a1..a3}, \code{battery-gate.rs\_v2}).
