\section{Background and Related Research}
\subsection{Executable CAD Representations}
DeepCAD represents a shape as a sequence of CAD operations and learns a Transformer-based model for autoencoding and generation \cite{deepcad}. Text2CAD conditions sequential parametric CAD generation on natural-language descriptions and evaluates visual, parametric and geometric outcomes \cite{text2cad}. These works make construction history part of the generated representation. Jarambit's retained crescent model instead decodes a fixed numerical schema into geometry. It does not learn a general CAD-operation language, and this study supplies no common benchmark against either method.

This difference matters when interpreting a genome. More numerical parameters do not necessarily express a new topology or construction strategy. A decoder revision can change the represented family without changing vector length. Representation identity therefore includes schema and decoder, not just gene count. The prospective agent research in Section IX concerns assistance with that representation; it is distinct from the implemented search over its parameters.

\subsection{Evolutionary Engineering and Diversity}
Evolutionary engineering predates language-model assistance. Lohn \emph{et al.}'s 2003 ST5 antenna report describes two evolutionary representations, fabricated prototypes and comparison with a hand-designed antenna \cite{st5}. Flight qualification was still pending in that report. Its relevance is the connection between representation, computational assessment and physical comparison, rather than evidence that Jarambit has achieved the same experimental maturity.

MAP-Elites organizes retained solutions by behavioral descriptors, preserving alternatives in different niches \cite{mapelites}. Scalar preference and diversity retention answer different questions: which candidate is preferred, and which alternatives remain available? An archive is selected by construction; its distribution is not the distribution of all proposals. Jarambit contains a quality-diversity lane, but the retained cohorts do not establish a common algorithm or descriptor regime across every wave.

\subsection{Agents as Proposal Mechanisms}
FunSearch separates language-model program proposals from execution-based evaluation in mathematical and algorithmic settings \cite{funsearch}. Text2CAD demonstrates text-conditioned CAD generation, but does not by that fact establish autonomous mechanical validation. Together these studies motivate a question about proposal interfaces and evaluators, not a demonstrated agent advantage in Jarambit. Table~\ref{tab:related} distinguishes their evaluation scopes.

An engineering agent could assist with schema documentation, source navigation and typed representation proposals. A proposed grammar or decoder revision also changes the evaluator's domain. Such a proposal needs review and independent assessment before its outcomes can be compared with the original representation. Section IX develops this prospective research boundary.

\subsection{Verification, Validation, and Identity}
Code verification examines implementation against reference problems; solution verification concerns numerical error for a specified model and observable; validation concerns agreement with the physical system in a declared domain. NASA's models-and-simulations standard supplies credibility context, without a claim of compliance here \cite{nasa}.

The assessment uses scikit-fem for finite-element assembly and a separate DOLFINx path \cite{skfem,dolfinx}. The DOLFINx methods reference describes the software architecture; the archived records identify the specific 0.9.0 environment. A software citation does not certify this model or establish that its outputs were independently reproduced. Matched solvers can share model and discretization errors, which makes their agreement a different observation from convergence or validation.

Content hashes bind bytes, while model identity, execution records and observable definitions supply their meaning. This distinction is essential when geometry, scorecards and fields originate from different revisions.

\subsection{Additive Manufacturing as Opportunity and Constraint}
NIST's additive-manufacturing test artifact addresses geometric characterization through manufactured features and measurements \cite{nistartifact}. It supports separating geometric expressiveness, nominal CAD precision and measured process capability. Additive manufacturing may make more complex forms practical to investigate; digital detail alone does not establish as-built accuracy.

This study's contribution lies between these research areas: it reconstructs how retained search rankings relate to subsequent numerical-assessment evidence in one executable CAD system. Learned CAD generation, agent advantage and manufacturing accuracy remain separate empirical questions.
