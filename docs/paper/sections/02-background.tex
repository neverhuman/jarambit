\section{Background and Related Research}
\subsection{Search, Diversity, and Representation}
Evolutionary design explores a representation by evaluating variants and using outcomes to influence subsequent proposals. A scalar objective answers which candidate is preferred under a particular aggregation of observations. A diversity mechanism asks which different kinds of candidate should remain available. These questions are related but distinct. A method can return a high scalar score while exploring only a narrow part of its representation.

MAP-Elites organizes retained solutions by user-selected behavioral descriptors, allowing high-performing alternatives to occupy different niches \cite{mapelites}. The relevance here is the distinction between a collection of elites and a population of evaluated births. An archive is selected by construction. Its distribution cannot be treated as an unbiased sample of all proposals, and its occupied cells do not establish that the surrounding space has been exhaustively explored. Jarambit contains a quality-diversity lane, but this paper does not claim that every retained wave uses the same algorithm or descriptor semantics.

Representation itself imposes an inductive bias. A parameter vector makes some changes easy to express and others impossible. Increasing the number of genes may enlarge the search space without making useful variation more accessible. A new decoder can produce a qualitatively different family even if the vector length is unchanged. Meaningful comparison therefore needs the representation version, decoder identity, and evaluator domain, not just a gene count.

\subsection{Agents as Proposal Mechanisms}
FunSearch demonstrates a separation between a language model that proposes programs and an evaluator that judges their outcomes in mathematical and algorithmic settings \cite{funsearch}. That separation motivates an engineering research question: could agents help propose more useful design representations while independent software and numerical checks constrain what can be claimed? The analogy is conceptual. Success in program search does not demonstrate success in CAD, mechanics, manufacturing, or this repository.

An engineering agent can assist with schema documentation, dimensional consistency, source navigation, test generation, and explanation. More ambitious proposals could change the representation grammar or decoder. Those proposals require stronger review because they can invalidate historical assumptions. The agent must not be treated as independent evidence for its own proposal, and fluent explanation must not substitute for an executed check. Section IX develops this boundary in operational terms at the software-research level.

\subsection{Verification, Validation, and Identity}
Code verification asks whether an implementation behaves as intended on suitable reference problems. Solution verification concerns numerical error for a specified model and observable. Model validation concerns agreement with the represented physical system within an identified domain. These activities support different claims. NASA's models-and-simulations standard provides a useful credibility context, but no compliance with that standard is asserted here \cite{nasa}.

Separate solvers can reveal implementation differences. Mesh studies can reveal discretization behavior. Neither automatically resolves errors in geometry, constitutive assumptions, supports, or observation definitions. Two solvers that share an incorrect problem specification may agree closely. A refined mesh may converge to a solution of an inadequate model. Conversely, an unfavorable numerical result can still be informative if its identity and uncertainty are well understood.

Identity is necessary throughout this chain. A content hash can establish that two references name the same bytes. It does not establish that those bytes represent the intended physical object, that a solver executed, or that a material property applies to a manufacturing process. The evidence chain therefore needs both content identity and semantic context. This distinction becomes especially important when a render, scorecard, CAD export, and stress field originate from different revisions.

\subsection{Additive Manufacturing as Opportunity and Constraint}
Additive manufacturing expands the geometric forms that can be produced directly from digital models. It also introduces process-dependent dimensional, surface, material, and inspection questions. NIST's test-artifact work addresses geometric characterization rather than assuming that digital precision transfers automatically to a printed part \cite{nistartifact}. The relevant promise is the ability to investigate more complex useful forms, coupled with measurement of what was actually made.

Accordingly, this paper separates geometric expressiveness, nominal CAD precision, numerical response, and measured manufacturing accuracy. An increase in the first does not prove an improvement in the others. The eventual scientific contribution of an agent-assisted CAD system would be strongest when those dimensions are measured together, including failures and uncertainty, on a declared application domain.
