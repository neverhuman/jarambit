\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the Retained Links Add}
The practical result is an inspectable distinction between a search preference and its supporting evidence. Parent links expose ancestor reuse; candidate and stage identities connect a stored ranking to a later assessment; source bindings keep selected numerical observations attached to their models. Missing observations remain visible rather than acquiring a favorable or unfavorable value. These capabilities explain the usefulness of the artifact without treating archive size or software size as proof of methodological novelty.

The numerical example makes the same point at another scale. Close agreement of selected observables on one shared solid discretization does not resolve the inadmissibility of a separate plate extrapolation. Both observations can be retained without collapsing them into one validation label. A derived view can help readers inspect such distinctions, but its source hashes alone do not establish the truth of the original computation.

\subsection{Threats to Interpretation}
The study is retrospective and confined to one repository. Prior workflows selected what was run and retained; completed runs are not a random sample of all attempts. Missing source files and later assessments restrict reconstruction, and missingness is not assumed random. Births share ancestry and search context, while numerical families can share geometry, fields, and assumptions. Record counts and family counts therefore do not count independent replications.

Contextual scores depend on the archive and population used to compute them. Cross-generation changes, per-run maxima, and cumulative best-so-far curves are descriptive observations rather than evidence of a common physical improvement trajectory. The historical assessment disagreement does not determine a population-wide failure rate. Open numerical questions, including the support eigenproblem audit, remain local limits on their associated observations.

Physical observations are not reported in this study. Material labels and CAD dimensions do not describe all relevant manufacturing state or measured process capability. NIST's test-artifact work illustrates why those properties require measurement \cite{nistartifact}. Retained computational mass and response values consequently do not establish the performance of a manufactured part.

\subsection{Questions Requiring New Evidence}
A controlled benign case could test how often search rankings survive an independently specified later assessment. A reporting study could measure whether independent readers reconstruct an observation correctly and detect provenance defects. Physical studies would need explicit specimens, conditions, metrology, and uncertainty. These prospective questions require new observations; the present contribution supplies a bounded evidence artifact from which to formulate them.
