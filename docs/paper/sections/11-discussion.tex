\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the Connections Establish}
The case study makes three relationships inspectable. Ancestry records connect a birth to recorded sources of variation; candidate and stage identities connect a search preference to a particular assessment; source artifacts connect displayed quantities to the models and transformations that produced them. The usefulness lies in being able to follow these relationships for a concrete result, rather than inferring them from a final geometry or score.

The historical comparison illustrates why the assessment predicate must travel with an outcome. A lower-ranked birth can meet the run's recorded predicate when the highest-ranked birth does not; that observation is meaningful within its stated selection and assessment context. The numerical comparison has an analogous structure: agreement on a shared solid discretization supports consistency of selected recovered observables, while the plate refinement study addresses admission of a different error estimate. Preserving both observations makes their respective implications visible.

\subsection{Scope and Limits}
This is a retrospective study of one repository and selected retained workflows. Prior selection and retention determine which observations are available, and missingness is not assumed random. Births share ancestry and search context; numerical observations can share geometry, preprocessing and model assumptions. Counts therefore describe record coverage, rather than independent experimental replication. Contextual fitness and cumulative best-so-far curves describe the recorded preference; neither those curves nor selected assessment comparisons estimate a population-wide qualification rate or establish comparative search advantage.

The mechanical observations are computational model outputs. Shared-input solver agreement leaves discretization, loading and boundary idealizations to their own checks; the support-study buckling estimate is not admitted because its operator/eigenpair assumptions remain unverified. Physical observations are not reported. Material labels and CAD dimensions also leave manufacturing state and process capability to measurement, as illustrated by NIST's test-artifact work \cite{nistartifact}. A visible motif and an authored feature family establish neither its autonomous invention nor an isolated engineering benefit. Those questions require additional evidence.

\subsection{A Testable Research Direction}
The next question is whether explicit links improve reconstruction and interpretation on benign scientific workflows. Controlled provenance defects could test detection and explanation, and independent readers could measure reconstruction accuracy, time and uncertainty. Such comparisons would evaluate the usefulness of the reporting approach itself. The present case supplies a concrete artifact and specific questions for that evaluation.

A second direction changes what is preferred rather than how it is recorded. A follow-on cohort, kept separate from every result in this paper, evolves the hand zone and the blade together under a different declared objective: ergonomics enters as a multiplier on the score, the grip may be hollow for additive manufacture, and the finger ring is sized for the loads a finger can apply rather than for the loads the battery applies. Its genome, decoder, objective and rules are versioned separately (schema \code{G3.0}, metric \code{W_ERGOLIGHT_v1}, rules \code{RS-karambit-v3}; source in \code{crates/g3}), and its candidates carry their own identifiers, so none of them can enter this paper's census. Whether the same evidence trail holds when the objective itself is the variable is the question that cohort is designed to answer; it reports no result here.
