\noindent\textbf{Abstract---}Automated design searches can retain extensive candidate records while leaving the evidence behind a selected result difficult to interpret. We present Jarambit as a retrospective study linking an application-specific geometric representation, recorded search observations, and candidate-specific numerical evidence. The largest archive contains 1.95 million search-evaluation records; historical genealogy and intermediate screening are analyzed separately because their scoring and retention regimes differ. Three findings delimit what these records establish. Evidence coverage is uneven: extensive genome and score tables do not imply retained parentage or downstream assessments. Exact content joins recover proposal occurrences without recovering parents. In the historical run, the highest stored search score fails an earlier Boolean predicate that a lower-ranked candidate satisfies; this is distinct from later qualification. For a separately assessed export, selected solid-model observables agree closely between implementations on a shared mesh, while separate plate-refinement sequences do not admit the chosen extrapolated-error estimates. The contribution is an inspectable connection between representation, candidate identity, assessment stage, and source artifacts, with incompatible scores and missing observations kept explicit. These distinctions make the reported evidence easier to question and reconstruct. An unsupported historical buckling inference is withdrawn while its original record is retained. The findings remain computational and retrospective; physical performance and general search advantage are unestablished.
