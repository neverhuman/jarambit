\section{Recorded Search and Inheritance}
\label{sec:evolution}

\subsection{Four Collections with Different Meanings}
Births, population memberships, archive representatives, and assessed exports answer different questions. Births record proposed individuals and their observations. Memberships record occupied island slots, including retained individuals. Quality-diversity archives retain representatives under a run's descriptor and score rules. Later assessments concern selectively admitted or separately exported candidates. Evidence coverage and denominators for these collections are reported with the cohort-specific results (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

For the historical cohort, the recorded membership stage is \emph{after contextual rescoring and before ring migration}. The bound producer emits birth or elite events and summary observations before migration. Migration subsequently copies selected IDs to destination islands; the overwritten destination IDs are not retained by those events. Calling the earlier snapshot a generation-end post-migration population would therefore overstate what can be reconstructed. Elite events retain membership but omit refreshed contextual fitness, so an individual's stored birth score is not substituted for an unrecorded later score.

\subsection{Variation in the Historical Producer}
The historical producer uses four island populations, shared novelty memory, a quality-diversity archive, elite retention, tournament selection, and periodic ring migration. Parents can be selected from the archive or current population; recorded parent IDs and operation labels identify the observed birth mechanism. These are source-bound historical semantics, rather than an assertion that every retained cohort executed an identical binary.

Crossover inherits the silhouette's 40 control points as one block and chooses the remaining numeric coordinates and material category independently between parents, then clamps the result. Mutation selects among six modes: sparse coordinate perturbation, a smooth silhouette bump, a thickness-and-section operator, a lattice operator, a fuller/texture/feature operator, and a machining-or-rail operator. Mutation scale depends on island and configuration; the exact parameter branches and the coarse/fine distinction are retained in Supplement~\ref{S-supp:representation-search}. This is variation within a fixed representation and decoder.

\subsection{Ancestor Reuse in the Retained Ledger}
Figure~\ref{fig:lineage} uses the current generated inheritance ledger's three-hop view. Individual~1095 has recorded parents 413 and 526, and 526 itself has parents 413 and 392. Individual~413 is therefore reused along the paths $413\to1095$ and $413\to526\to1095$. The dashed $174\to299$ link is a recorded relationship retained as induced closure, distinct from the three-hop edges. The ledger preserves exact coordinate differences and parent ordinals; the main figure emphasizes relationships rather than repeating their raw mixed-unit magnitudes.

This ancestry illustrates a reconstruction capability. Its focal birth's high stored search score and later assessment disagreement are separate observations, developed in Section~\ref{sec:results}; neither ancestry nor score alone establishes physical performance.
