\subsection{An Authored Feature Vocabulary}
The documented representation provides silhouette and opening controls alongside longitudinal channels, face relief, patterned pockets, perforations and ring grooves. The producer's genome and phenotype sources define these families explicitly, including a blade fuller, triangular isogrid pockets, lattice features and ligament flutes; the reference source separately defines a ring-face groove\cite{repo}. These are authored parts of the vocabulary available to the decoder, rather than feature families whose invention is demonstrated by the illustrated export.

This vocabulary gives ordinary names to otherwise abstract coordinates. A fuller names a channel family; a pocket names a recessed region; a perforation names a through-opening. Material remaining between openings can be described as a bridge without assigning it a particular structural cross-section. Conditional switches, coupled bounds and construction rules determine how stored settings become geometry (Section~\ref{sec:genome}). The available vocabulary and an individual feature's realization are therefore distinct observations.

\subsection{Occurrence Requires an Identified Geometry}
An occurrence claim belongs to a particular export and decoder context. The appropriate evidence is the retained geometry with its candidate identity and source binding. A nonzero gene setting alone does not establish the realized shape. Likewise, the boundaries in a response-colored image identify changes in a displayed field; they are not measurements of a geometric cross-section.

Neutral geometry and response views should consequently be read together, with a consistent orientation and an explicit account of the pictured region. Additional local anatomy callouts require a bound geometry asset and crop transformation. Until those bindings are supplied, descriptive names remain pointers for inspection rather than authenticated measurements of the highlighted regions. A content match can identify a recorded occurrence; a nearby genome does not establish a parent or the history of an individual feature.

\subsection{Utility Is a Separate Engineering Claim}
The saved assessments concern the illustrated candidate under their stated models and loads. They do not isolate the contribution of one channel, pocket or opening. This section therefore describes the representation and its inspectable artifacts without assigning feature-specific mass savings, efficiency, optimality or manufacturing qualification. Its value is explanatory: readers can connect the numeric representation to a concrete object while keeping feature availability, observed occurrence and demonstrated utility separate.
