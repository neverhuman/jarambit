\label{sec:fitness}

Their names, directions, observation stages and missing-input rules must accompany their values. Table~\ref{tab:score-dictionary} keeps the score regimes separate; a shared word such as ``fitness'' does not create a common performance axis.

\subsection{A Retained Weighted Reporting Vector}
The principal reporting definition is
\begin{equation}
 F=\sum_{i=1}^{20}w_i s_i,\qquad s_i\in[0,1],\quad \sum_iw_i=100.
\end{equation}
Its priorities are mass/compactness (30\%), form/comfort proxies (25\%), exported geometry (15\%) and response/verification (30\%). Table~\ref{tab:weighted-priorities} shows sums of \emph{stored weighted contributions} for Individual~2002. The Supplement retains all twenty normalized values, weights and contributions. These are reported observations from its summary, not a recalculation under the present scorer and not measurements transferred from Individual~2000.

\subsection{Context and Display Direction}
Native fitness includes novelty computed against contemporaneous population/archive descriptors. A value can therefore change when its context changes, even if the genome does not. Birth scores and later membership observations also have distinct stages. Cross-generation or cross-run maxima describe retained contextual preference; they do not isolate a physical improvement. The historical and newer native implementations/configurations require separate provenance even when both call their scalar $f$.

A display loss $L=-f$, $100-F$, or $-W_{\mathrm{CAD}}$ reverses numerical order. Minimizing it preserves the original preference for a larger native score. It does not make those different profiles comparable. A cumulative maximum is monotone by construction, so its corresponding loss is nonincreasing by construction; that fact alone is not evidence of continuing physical improvement.

\subsection{Missing-Aware Response Display and Physical-Unit Views}
The display roster accounts for all $44$ catalog predicates and $8$ retained-channel metadata rows. These $52$ metadata rows are not $52$ independent tests or performed solves. Four distinct retained unit-response channels of Individual~2000 share the original plate triangles: lateral, in-plane tip pry, ring traction and chord torsion. The scaled envelope and its derived safety-factor map remain companion quantities, without another equal weight. Scalar-only, missing or incompatible-domain observations remain missing spatial contributions; no scalar or Individual~2002 reporting term is broadcast onto this geometry.

Within each eligible channel, the normalization is an ascending area-weighted percentile midrank of valid original response values. All exactly tied values share the midpoint of their pooled area; a constant valid field has rank $0.5$. Original source masks apply, while genuine finite zeros remain valid. For cell $i$, let $K_i$ contain the available normalized channels. The unweighted display index and its support are
\begin{equation}
 c_i=\frac{1}{q_i}\sum_{k\in K_i}r_{ki},\qquad q_i=|K_i|>0.
\end{equation}
An all-missing cell has no index and $q_i=0$, rather than index zero. Contributor identities and coverage $q_i/4$ accompany the index; $44$ and $52$ are inventory counts, not its support denominator. No candidate-specific minimum/maximum stretch follows the mean. A high index denotes relatively high response within the available channels, not safety, fitness, likelihood or merit.

Physical values, within-channel normalization, the available-channel mean and display contrast are separate quantities. The full-range quantitative companion (Supplement Figure~\ref{S-fig:hero-quantitative}) uses a shared flat navy--teal--yellow--orange--red transfer for surface and legend over $[0,1]$; this palette does not claim monotone luminance. Lighting in the main appearance view changes RGB values without changing the underlying index or retained responses. A percentile-window contrast view is a separate coordinate with declared index bounds, saturation counts and source-area fractions, accompanied by the full-range view. The Supplement specifies the roster, masks, normalization and projection limits.

Figure~\ref{fig:plate-fields} gives source-coordinate views of the envelope and three physical-unit responses; the fourth eligible response, in-plane tip pry, appears in Figure~\ref{fig:saliency-crops}C. Its display policy chooses a concentrated upper-value zoom only when whole-field coverage, compactness and scale-gain requirements are met; otherwise it retains full context. An upper-value cutoff is a fraction of the original eligible value range, not a count or area quantile. Gray denotes producer-masked cells, not measured zero stress. Raw torsion retains its tip-patch peak, distinct from the post-exclusion peak used by selected capacity calculations.
