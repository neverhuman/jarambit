\section{Score Definitions and Comparability}
\label{sec:fitness}
A search preference, a stored reporting vector, a shortfall score and a mechanical safety factor answer different questions. Their names, directions, observation stages and missing-input rules must accompany their values. Table~\ref{tab:score-dictionary} keeps the score regimes separate; a shared word such as ``fitness'' does not create a common performance axis.

\subsection{A Retained Weighted Reporting Vector}
The principal reporting definition is
\begin{equation}
 F=\sum_{i=1}^{20}w_i s_i,\qquad s_i\in[0,1],\quad \sum_iw_i=100.
\end{equation}
Its priorities are mass/compactness (30\%), form/comfort proxies (25\%), exported geometry (15\%) and response/verification (30\%). Table~\ref{tab:weighted-priorities} shows sums of \emph{stored weighted contributions} for Individual~2002. The Supplement retains all twenty normalized values, weights and contributions. These are reported observations from its summary, not a recalculation under the present scorer and not measurements transferred from Individual~2000.

\subsection{Context and Display Direction}
Native fitness includes novelty computed against contemporaneous population/archive descriptors. A value can therefore change when its context changes, even if the genome does not. Birth scores and later membership observations also have distinct stages. Cross-generation or cross-run maxima describe retained contextual preference; they do not isolate a physical improvement. The historical and newer native implementations/configurations require separate provenance even when both call their scalar $f$.

A display loss $L=-f$, $100-F$, or $-W_{\mathrm{CAD}}$ reverses numerical order. Minimizing it preserves the original preference for a larger native score. It does not make those different profiles comparable. A cumulative maximum is monotone by construction, so its corresponding loss is nonincreasing by construction; that fact alone is not evidence of continuing physical improvement.

\subsection{Relative Field Summary and Physical-Unit Views}
The hero summarizes retained fields of Individual~2000. For each active map, cells are sorted by stored magnitude and assigned the midpoint of their cumulative area interval. Equal stored values are processed in deterministic source-cell order, rather than pooled into one tied percentile. The active-map ranks are averaged at each cell, then the candidate's resulting minimum and maximum are linearly stretched to $[0,1]$ for display. This candidate-specific relative summary removes physical units. A color value of $0.8$ means neither $80\%$ safety nor an absolute $80$th percentile shared by other candidates.

The five maps are the envelope, lateral, pry, ring and torsion arrays. Because the envelope is derived from the primitive responses, five contributors do not represent five independent tests. Ring/envelope masks vary the number of active contributors; the cover does not display a separate per-cell support map. Section~\ref{sec:verification} and the Supplement describe the actual support counts and primitive-only sensitivity.

Figure~\ref{fig:plate-fields} retains four physical-unit maps; the fifth, in-plane tip pry, appears in Figure~\ref{fig:saliency-crops}C. Gray denotes producer-masked cells, not measured zero stress. Raw torsion has a large tip-patch peak, which remains distinct from the post-exclusion peak used by selected capacity calculations.
