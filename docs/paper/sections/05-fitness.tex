\section{Fitness Objective and Its Engineering Diagnostics}
\subsection{What the Existing Weighted Score Means}
The retained weighted reporting score has the generic form
\begin{equation}
 F=\sum_{i=1}^{20}w_i s_i,\qquad 0\leq s_i\leq1,\quad \sum_iw_i=100.
\end{equation}
Weights are expressed in percentage points, so $F$ lies on a 0--100 scale and higher is better. This equation describes the aggregation, not proof that each $s_i$ is independently measured, smooth, or physically calibrated. Some components are clipped ratios, Boolean contributions, constants, or proxies. Calling the aggregate continuous should not obscure discontinuities in its inputs or the distinction between continuous-valued output and differentiable behavior.

The selected reporting profile groups the existing terms into mass/compactness, form/comfort proxies, exported geometry, and response/verification. These are explanatory groupings of the retained terms, not newly calibrated objectives. The profile's total reproduces the stored best score. Its labels do not establish subjective comfort, clinical safety, physical performance, or manufacturing readiness.

\subsection{Scorer Identity Is Part of the Result}
Native Stage 1 uses a reduced set of observations plus fixed and proxy contributions. Native Stage 2 uses a richer result structure, but still maps several terms to constants or reused quantities. The separate Python reporting scorer consumes another set of inputs and, at this baseline, uses disclosed placeholder values for two geometry inputs. It can therefore produce a score that differs from the native selection function even when the metric name appears similar.

The paper's opening profile is explicitly a \emph{retained reporting profile}. Its source merges selected-row screening metadata with a substituted assessment profile: for example, the outer and assessed sequence/fatigue observations differ. These differences may be legitimate fidelity effects, but the combined record cannot establish one scorer/stage for every trajectory point. We preserve the source values, label the lineage incomplete, and make no convergence claim from this series. A common metric name or a monotonic envelope is insufficient to establish comparability.

The historical population uses another score entirely. Its admitted producer applies legality-dependent branches and includes novelty calculated from the contemporaneous archive/population. We summarize saved \code{fit} values without rescoring candidates. The source-defined context, evaluation status, and later assessment outcome must accompany interpretation; an increasing recorded maximum alone does not establish improved physical response.

\subsection{Requested Minimization Convention}
The requested future product direction is a continuously reported weighted objective whose lower value indicates improvement. The present retained evidence instead uses maximization. A display transform $L=100-F$ would reverse direction and preserve ordering within the same scorer version. It would not correct placeholders, remove clipping, calibrate uncertainty, create new observations, or establish that engineering requirements are satisfied. This manuscript and its implementation do not activate a new physical objective.

The distinction between preference and evidential validity remains essential. Soft ranking can preserve an informative but unfavorable candidate for research. It cannot make missing solver evidence valid or turn a provisional record into a qualified design. Qualification and physical validation require their own explicit evidence, independently of the scalar used to allocate research attention.

\subsection{How to Read the Following Tests}
The following subsections explain every legacy CAD diagnostic. Exact predicate keys and source links appear in the online atlas. The predicate suffixes are historical identities; they are not new load recommendations. The authoritative predicate map remains in \src{ops/ci/evaluate_cad_candidate.py}{the existing evaluator}. We describe observation semantics, shared dependencies and interpretation limits instead of creating a second, drifting authority for requirement constants.

\test{G16-01: Export Topology and Identity}
This diagnostic concerns the assessed export's relation to the intended candidate and the topology accepted by the existing export checks. It reads the recorded CAD-validity result rather than inferring validity from a rendered image. Relevant evidence includes exported artifact identity, solid count, topology analysis and conversion context. A watertight triangle shell and a valid B-rep solid are different statements: each representation has its own checks and failure modes. A favorable topology field does not establish unit identity, physical manufacture, or agreement of all mass representations. Those questions require their own observations and bindings.

\test{G16-02: Geometry and Holder Compatibility}
The geometry predicate collects existing representation and holder-related checks. Its inputs include recorded dimensions, envelope relationships and rejection reasons. This is a deterministic geometric assessment under a defined decoder and holder model. It is useful for detecting a candidate that violates the model's representational assumptions, but it does not measure retention during motion, fit across a human population, or contact pressure. Reports should retain the individual rejection reasons instead of exposing only a Boolean, because different geometric mismatches have different implications for downstream model applicability.

\test{G16-03: Shape and Archetype}
This predicate describes conformity to the represented shape family, using ring and curve descriptors computed from the phenotype. Its value depends on the decoder and feature definitions. It therefore belongs to representation checking rather than physical validation. A favorable result means that the encoded family conditions were reported as met under that version. It cannot establish that the shape is functionally superior, comfortable or appropriate for an operational use. If a future representation changes the shape family, this predicate's definition must be reconsidered explicitly rather than carried forward by name alone.

\test{G16-04: Reference-Shape Comparison}
The aesthetic/reference diagnostic compares geometry with the stored reference and associated feature checks. Metrics such as distance or distribution similarity are automated descriptors whose meaning depends on normalization, correspondence and reference identity. They can make a search's appearance bias reproducible. They cannot replace human aesthetic approval or an ergonomic study. The distinction matters when automated similarity is given a positive score contribution: a scalar preference for reference resemblance is a design choice, not an empirical finding about user experience.

\test{G16-05: Lateral-Response Comparison}
This predicate compares a retained plate-model lateral response with one legacy requirement. The underlying observation is obtained from the recorded continuum model and material envelope; the comparison is a postprocessing statement about that observation. Its interpretation requires the correct region, stress recovery, discretization and model revision. The historical use-case name should not be read as a validated operational load specification. In particular, this predicate and the next reuse a response family. Counting their two Boolean outcomes as two independent engineering experiments would inflate the evidence.

\test{G16-06: Additional Lateral Comparison}
The second lateral predicate applies another legacy comparison to the related retained response. Its usefulness lies in showing how one modeled observation relates to a different requirement scope. It does not create an additional solver execution or an independently measured capacity. A report should display the observation once, with the separate comparisons clearly attached, so readers can see the dependency. Apparent agreement between the two predicates provides little additional confidence when both are derived from the same inputs and numerical field.

\test{G16-07: Refined Lateral Observation}
This diagnostic uses the refined-discretization lateral observation rather than silently substituting the cheaper screening result. Its field identity includes the refinement convention in the existing implementation. A smaller element size may change the result, but refinement by itself does not establish an asymptotic error regime. Geometry, material, supports, load definition and the observable must remain matched across a study. If the refined observation is unavailable, the correct public state is unavailable or unverified. A coarse favorable screen cannot stand in for a missing refined assessment.

\test{G16-08: Ring-Traction Response}
The ring-traction predicate reads the existing static model's response comparison. As with other modeled capacities, its meaning depends on the represented geometry, constitutive assumption, support and stress observable. A favorable numerical comparison is not a physical proof test. Nor does it establish an injury criterion or the response of a person in contact with the part. The historical diagnostic is valuable as a documented model question, provided the report preserves that scope and does not turn its legacy name into a broad practical-safety claim.

\test{G16-09: Body-Region Torsion}
The body torsion diagnostic reads a regional observable from the existing model. The region definition is part of the result: excluding or including a local zone can materially alter which stress values enter the comparison. Stress recovery and averaging conventions also matter. A report therefore needs the region mask, observable type, source field and model identity, not merely a safety-factor number. This body observation should remain separate from local plate and local solid observations even when all derive from a related loading family.

\test{G16-10: Local Plate Torsion}
This predicate evaluates the existing local region in the plate representation. Its local character does not make it a separate physical experiment, and its plate formulation does not resolve every three-dimensional stress effect. The result can expose a deficiency in the modeled region while still requiring a different model to examine local solid behavior. A viewer should not compare its scalar directly with a solid-element scalar without checking region, recovery and normalization. These details define whether the two numbers answer the same engineering question.

\test{G16-11: Refined Local Torsion}
The refined local predicate reads the corresponding retained refined observation. It is a distinct evidence requirement even when a coarse value exists. Missing refined evidence must remain explicit. The source records should identify how the local region was transferred, how the observable was recovered, and whether geometry and model assumptions were preserved. Refinement changes numerical resolution; it does not, by itself, validate a support assumption or establish a fracture mechanism. This diagnostic is therefore a more specific numerical observation, not an automatic escalation to physical truth.

\test{G16-12: Quadratic Local Solid Result}
The local solid path uses quadratic tetrahedral elements, with a native Rust screen and separate assessment tooling. Its result refers to a local three-dimensional submodel, not necessarily the whole part. Boundary transfer, mesh identity and the selected stress observable are essential to interpretation. A nodal maximum is not interchangeable with a cell value, an integration-point maximum, or an averaged field. A complete result record can contain an unfavorable comparison. Solver completion, numerical verification and physical validation are three further questions, each requiring its own evidence.

\test{G16-13: Support and Stability}
The alternate-support diagnostic records the existing compliant-support representation and its linear buckling observation. A generalized eigenvalue describes a modeled stability relationship under a particular linearization; it does not establish nonlinear postbuckling response, imperfections, contact evolution or physically calibrated support stiffness. Reports should preserve both the support-model identity and execution status. A favorable eigenvalue is informative within that model. It should not be converted into a broad claim that a real grip, interface or manufactured part has been validated.

\test{G16-14: Regional Yield-Order Proxy}
The sequence diagnostic compares regional elastic response limits under the existing convention. It is intended to distinguish which modeled region reaches its specified limit first. The ratio is an elastic proxy. It does not simulate crack initiation, propagation, plastic collapse, dynamic fracture or human injury. Its numerator and denominator must retain region and load-case identities; reusing a ratio from another case would change its meaning. A favorable proxy therefore supports a narrow model statement rather than a demonstrated physical failure sequence.

\test{G16-15: Mesh and Observable Consistency}
This diagnostic checks whether the recorded mesh sequence and observable identities support a stated convergence comparison. Arithmetic can be correct while the study is semantically invalid: meshes may not form the intended sequence, regions may differ, or observations may use different field recovery. Valid metadata is necessary but does not alone admit a discretization-error estimate. Divergent order, a plateau, sign behavior and insufficient separation can limit interpretation. The report should distinguish metadata validity, computed quantities and the final admission decision rather than compressing them into one reassuring label.

\test{G16-16: Separate-Solver Consistency}
The cross-solver diagnostic compares separately attributed outputs for a matched discretized problem. The evidence should identify both executions, solver versions, mesh bytes, boundary conditions and observable definitions. Close agreement can reveal implementation consistency, but shared assumptions can produce shared error. An output field copied into another record is not an independent execution. A matched-mesh comparison also does not measure geometry-model or discretization uncertainty by itself. The strongest statement supported by this diagnostic remains scoped agreement of the specified computations.

\subsection{The Ten Overlapping RS-v2 Groups}
The grouped requirement implementation is \src{crates/cli/src/bench.rs}{\code{rs_v2_eval}}. These groups reuse observations from the legacy diagnostics and add other model or geometry comparisons. Ten groups plus 16 predicates do not constitute 26 independent experiments. Their exact IDs remain stable even if a presentation changes row order.

\test{RSV2-01: Shape and Geometry}
The \code{aesthetic_and_geometric} group combines the existing shape/reference and geometric findings. Its conjunctive result should preserve the underlying reasons for any unfavorable or missing component. It shares observations with the corresponding G16 checks and inherits their limits concerning human fit, appearance and model scope.

\test{RSV2-02: Combined Numerical Response}
The \code{floors_828_joint} group combines response comparisons under the recorded material-envelope convention. A grouped outcome does not add a new solve for every constituent Boolean. Its interpretation requires the original observation identities and a clear statement of which comparisons are present or missing.

\test{RSV2-03: Body and Refined Local Torsion}
The \code{twist_floor_body_and_tipsub_strict} group retains both regional comparisons. One favorable body observation must not conceal an unfavorable or unavailable local result. Region identity and refinement scope remain part of the evidence, even when the group is rendered as a single outcome.

\test{RSV2-04: Local Solid Response}
The \code{solid_tip_sf} group compares the local-solid observation. It does not replace checks of geometry binding, independent execution or discretization. A result may be complete and numerically unfavorable; both facts should remain visible rather than being compressed into a generic successful-test label.

\test{RSV2-05: Refined Lateral Response}
The \code{strict_hunter_lateral} group reuses the refined lateral observation. Its historical name remains an identifier, not a newly established operational scenario. It inherits the refined model's assumptions and uncertainty, and must not be counted independently of G16-07 when discussing evidence volume.

\test{RSV2-06: Regional Yield Order}
The \code{seq_hard} group reuses the regional yield-order proxy. The legacy word ``hard'' describes a comparison policy in that requirement namespace. It does not turn the elastic proxy into a validated fracture mechanism, nor does it dictate how a separate research-selection scalar must be interpreted.

\test{RSV2-07: Additional Response and Geometry}
The \code{human_max} group combines additional response and geometry observations specified by the existing implementation. Its name is not a validated bound on human behavior. The constituent fields and their origins are necessary to understand an unfavorable result; a synthetic or placeholder geometry input cannot establish a measured human-interface property.

\test{RSV2-08: Fatigue Model}
The \code{fatigue} group reads the existing fatigue-screen observation. Mean-stress treatment, material state, surface assumptions and load-spectrum interpretation affect its meaning. A modeled factor is not a measured service life. Transfer to a different process or surface condition requires evidence rather than a repeated material name.

\test{RSV2-09: Exported Edge Geometry}
The \code{gate_s_sharp_law} group assesses recorded exported geometric features. It is a geometry comparison, not an experiment in cutting effectiveness or durability. Nominal decoder intent and measurements on the actual exported representation should remain distinct, especially when export construction changes across revisions.

\test{RSV2-10: Exported Local Profile}
The \code{gate_s_tip_point_brep} group assesses the exported local profile and its recorded monotonicity. It cannot establish manufactured dimensions or practical performance. The relevant output needs geometry identity and measurement semantics; an attractive image or a parameter's nominal value cannot substitute for those observations.
