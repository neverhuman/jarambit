\section{Introduction: Tracing Search to Evidence}
\label{sec:introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{utomated} design searches can produce more candidate records than a reader can inspect individually. A selected geometry and its final score reveal little about which representation produced it, which objective ranked it, or whether later assessments support the same conclusion. Interpreting a computational design result therefore requires links between the candidate, its search context, and the evidence actually retained for it.

We use \emph{Engineered Intelligence} for a design process connecting a versioned representation, a geometric decoder, recorded variation and selection, declared objectives, and inspectable observations. Jarambit provides a retrospective case study of these connections in an application-specific titanium CAD system. Its representation has 128 numeric coordinates, including discretized selectors, plus a categorical material field. The analyzed records come from workflows with different scorers and retention policies; they do not form a single uniform experiment.

\subsection{Three Questions and Findings}
\label{subsec:research-question-and-results}
This study asks three questions about what the retained records establish:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item What can a reader reconstruct about a generated individual and the search context that ranked it?
  \item Which evidence is retained for each cohort, and what is observed when an individual reaches later assessment?
  \item What do selected numerical agreement and mesh-study observations establish, and what remains unresolved?
\end{enumerate}

Three findings answer these questions. First, evidence coverage is uneven: the large native-search archive retains compact genome and score records, while recorded parentage belongs to a smaller historical cohort. Neither collection's size establishes downstream assessment coverage. Second, the historical genealogy exposes ancestor reuse, and its highest stored search score belongs to a birth that fails a later internal assessment. This is a documented assessment disagreement; the retained example does not determine a general failure rate or a pairwise rank reversal. Third, selected solid-model observables agree closely between two implementations on a shared mesh, while separate plate-refinement sequences do not admit the chosen extrapolated-error estimates. These observations answer different numerical questions.

The historical genealogy, intermediate screening campaign, completed native runs, and separately assessed exports are therefore reported with their own identities and evidence coverage. In particular, the illustrated assessed export, Individual~2000, supplies the spatial fields; Individual~2002 supplies a retained weighted reporting-profile record whose exported CAD is unavailable. Their observations are not interchangeable. Section~\ref{sec:results} develops the cohort-specific findings, and Section~\ref{sec:verification} interprets the numerical evidence.

\subsection{Three Bounded Contributions}
\label{subsec:contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Representation and identity links.} A semantic genome contract and distinct identities for births, memberships, and assessed artifacts let readers ask how a recorded individual was produced. The historical ancestry example demonstrates reuse across branches rather than an assumed branching tree (Sections~\ref{sec:genome}--\ref{sec:evolution}).
  \item \textbf{Retrospective interpretation across assessment stages.} Keeping score regimes and missing observations separate lets readers ask which conclusions a retained ranking supports. The historical search-to-assessment disagreement and explicit unscreened proposals demonstrate why a preferred, unobserved, or assessed candidate denotes a different evidentiary state (Sections~\ref{sec:fitness} and~\ref{sec:results}).
  \item \textbf{Source-bound evidence views and coverage accounting.} Linked records, field views, and reporting artifacts let readers inspect the source and scope of a displayed result. The shared-mesh comparison and refused plate extrapolations demonstrate how distinct evidence can be presented without turning it into a single validation claim (Sections~\ref{sec:verification}--\ref{sec:reproducibility}).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Study scope.} This is a retrospective study of retained computational evidence. Internal assessment labels describe the recorded workflow; physical observations are not reported in this study. The records do not establish comparative optimization superiority, physical qualification, or transfer to other design domains. Reporting reconstruction and numerical solver replay remain distinct operations.
