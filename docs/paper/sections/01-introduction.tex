\section{Introduction: Intelligence in the Design Loop}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{n} engineer usually begins with an intelligible shape, changes a manageable set of dimensions, and evaluates the consequences. Computational design permits a different order: specify a representation, define observations and preferences, and explore many admissible variations before selecting a shape for closer study. The intellectual shift is substantial. A designer can reason about a family of possible objects rather than committing early to a single object. Yet the family, the evaluator, and the definition of improvement remain human engineering choices.

We use \emph{intelligent computational design} for the coordinated use of representations, search, numerical models, software agents and human judgment. Its scientific value depends on what the process learns about candidate behavior and how those observations can be checked.

Jarambit provides a useful case study because it contains more engineering machinery than its front-page story readily conveys. Its current crescent representation has 128 numerical genes and a material field. A decoded candidate is evaluated through plate and local-solid models, geometric diagnostics, requirement comparisons, software controls, and retained evidence records. A browser studio presents CAD and scorecards. Different search lanes coexist, and historical scoring conventions remain visible in the repository \cite{repo}. The depth is real, but the categories require careful explanation.

Three questions organize this paper. First, how does a numerical gene become an identifiable CAD artifact with interpretable observations? Second, what do the engineering checks establish, and do retained search rankings and verification results justify stronger assessment claims? Third, what evidence would be needed to support stronger claims about agent-assisted representation design, continuous optimization, manufacturing precision, and practical value? These questions separate a system's architecture from the empirical claims that can currently be defended.

\subsection{Contributions and Evidence Boundary}
The first contribution is an end-to-end explanation of the existing representation, search, assessment, and reporting lifecycle. The second is a complete taxonomy of the CAD diagnostics and battery evaluators, including their overlaps and interpretation limits. The third is a reproducible retrospective case analysis: the highest historical search score fails later assessment; valid plate-study metadata coexist with refused GCI estimates; and the separately retained solid comparison reports close scalar agreement. A fixed historical battery and one weighted cohort supply additional reporting context. The fourth is a research agenda for agent-assisted genome design that preserves the independence of proposal and evaluation.

The repository's original application is a titanium utility-knife research study. This retrospective analysis documents its computational assessment architecture; it introduces no new geometry, physical tests, optimization method or manufacturing experiment. Broader civilian applications are prospective rather than demonstrated transfers of qualification.

\subsection{Why This Is a Research Problem}
As search becomes cheaper, the number of proposed designs can grow much faster than the amount of trustworthy evidence about each one. When representations become more expressive, an evaluator may be applied outside the domain for which its assumptions were reasonable. When agents generate changes to code or schemas, a search can alter the meaning of the very variables being optimized. These are research problems in experiment design, numerical credibility, and scientific reporting as much as in optimization.

Our central proposition is that representation freedom should be paired with evidence precision. Candidate identity, observation and interpretation must remain connected as the represented family and evaluator evolve. The retrospective results show where a search ranking, a favorable legacy flag or close solver agreement would otherwise suggest a stronger conclusion than its underlying evidence supports.

The remainder proceeds from representation and search to the fitness and test taxonomy, then numerical verification, retained results, agents, reproducibility, and future manufacturing applications. The online atlas supplies exact source links for all named CAD entries. The paper supplies the conceptual and technical connections needed to read that atlas as a coherent engineering system.
