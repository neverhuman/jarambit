\section{Three Findings from the Retained Records}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Large Record Coverage Does Not Imply Rich Assessment Coverage}
The ten completed native runs (Runs~2--11) retain $1{,}954{,}560$ evaluated birth records, $195{,}456$ per run across generations $0$--$800$. The admission receipts separately count $32{,}040$ generation/island summary rows. All birth rows preserve the numeric coordinates and material, but no per-birth later CAD/solid assessment is retained for this population. The largest archive therefore supplies search-record coverage rather than a qualification rate.

Figure~\ref{fig:population-latest} keeps the native population separate from the historical $1984$ births and the intermediate campaign's $2259$ numeric screens. The $1390$ proposals lacking screens have no invented score. Within the native archive, $754{,}236$ births ($38.59\%$) are search-legal; $610{,}051$ of those have negative contextual fitness. Score sign consequently cannot stand in for legality. Per-run legal counts range from $70{,}278$ to $80{,}132$, with mean $75{,}424$ and sample standard deviation $2937$ births. Run-maximum stored fitness ranges from $1.1089$ to $1.1440$ (mean $1.1250$, sample standard deviation $0.01285$). These are descriptive summaries of ten selected runs; related births do not provide millions of independent replicates.

\subsection{Recorded Ancestry Enables a Later-Assessment Disagreement to Be Inspected}
The historical Run~1 retains $1984$ births, recorded within-run parent links, $2176$ island-slot memberships and $12$ migration events. Parent identifiers for external seed ancestry are not recorded. The genealogy exposes reuse of ancestors rather than a simple branching tree. Its saved FEM-status categories distinguish $1455$ complete records from $529$ not evaluated; the latter are not failed solves.

Individual~1095 has the highest stored historical birth score ($f=1.15727$, generation~9). Its later strict-assessment summary records \code{cert\_pass\_all=false} and \code{seq\_ok=false}, although its strict lateral predicate is true. Individual~1543 has the eighth-highest stored birth score ($f=1.15415$, generation~13), yet its summary records \code{cert\_pass\_all=true} and \code{seq\_ok=true}. Thus a lower-ranked birth meets the earlier historical certification predicate that the highest-ranked birth fails. This categorical preference--predicate disagreement is not a continuous later-score rank reversal, and that historical predicate is not the later 26-predicate qualification definition.

These are extrema of retained observations under their native preference directions, not newly evaluated designs. The native maximum has seven ties at serialized precision; its earliest original ID and existing public alias are reported without promoting a qualified champion. The source-bound retained-leader receipt preserves every exact tie and each run's own context. Producer/configuration equivalence across the ten native runs is not admitted, including five runs without retained configuration files. Contextual native fitness, screening deficits, weighted reporting summaries and later assessment scores also have different producer and observation-stage bindings. Their values do not establish a single comparable global best. Missing downstream evidence is not a failed assessment, and missing CAD for a score leader does not make the illustrated example a score winner.

The intermediate campaign retains $3649$ proposals in 57 birth files across 24 run directories. One-based candidate indexing joins $2259$ screens; $1390$ screen observations are missing. A separate retained weighted-reporting subset has six curve files and fifteen generation summaries. In Run~31, the archive score changes from $74.1675$ (Individual~2009) to $74.2073$ (Individual~2002), then stays at that value. An archive maximum is monotone by construction. The curve alone does not show continuing improvement in physical quality. A compatible versioned weighted recipe is recoverable, but the curve does not establish its exact executed producer or uniform observation stage and is not a controlled convergence experiment.

The automatic leaderboard separately retains one assessed export, Individual~2000, with zero qualified and no promoted champion. The historical $23\times18$ battery includes shared controls, blocked bindings and completeness flags; it is not a set of 414 independent mechanical tests. Individual~2002 also has a retained later-assessment summary: $W_{\mathrm{CAD}}=-7.414831$, $G_1=0.259798625$, solid-tip factor $0.3636$, 15 battery entries passing and \code{qualified=false}. Its underlying exported CAD and full scorecard files are not retained at the referenced paths in the checked repository tree. Its linked proposals and screens are retained in another accession. The earlier screen label ``pending independent top-k assessment'' is not its final retained assessment-summary status. Neither that summary nor its weighted vector supplies the geometry or response fields of Individual~2000.

\subsection{Selected Solver Agreement and Refused Plate Extrapolation Support Different Conclusions}
On the same saved solid mesh, the scikit-fem/PyAMG and DOLFINx/PETSc paths agree within $0.05\%$ for the two retained recovered safety-factor observables. Separately, the plate mesh sequence does not admit its selected extrapolated error estimates: one observed order is nonpositive and the other sequence oscillates. Section~\ref{sec:verification} gives the inputs, recovery definitions and refusal reasons. Close shared-input agreement does not repair the extrapolation assumptions or establish physical qualification.
