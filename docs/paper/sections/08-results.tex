\section{Three Findings from the Retained Records}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Large Record Coverage Does Not Imply Rich Assessment Coverage}
The ten completed native runs (Runs~2--11) retain $1{,}954{,}560$ evaluated birth records, $195{,}456$ per run across generations $0$--$800$. The admission receipts separately count $32{,}040$ generation/island summary rows. All birth rows preserve the numeric coordinates and material, but no per-birth later CAD/solid assessment is retained for this population. The largest archive therefore supplies search-record coverage rather than a qualification rate.

Figure~\ref{fig:population-latest} keeps the native population separate from the historical $1984$ births and the intermediate campaign's $2259$ numeric screens. The $1390$ proposals lacking screens have no invented score. Within the native archive, $754{,}236$ births ($38.59\%$) are search-legal; $610{,}051$ of those have negative contextual fitness. Score sign consequently cannot stand in for legality. Per-run legal counts range from $70{,}278$ to $80{,}132$, with mean $75{,}424$ and sample standard deviation $2937$ births. Run-maximum stored fitness ranges from $1.1089$ to $1.1440$ (mean $1.1250$, sample standard deviation $0.01285$). These are descriptive summaries of ten selected runs; related births do not provide millions of independent replicates.

Those panels contain $1{,}919{,}651$ point operations and omit the numeric tails, so the overview's zero-exclusion claim does not apply to them. Separate per-run point and retained-best context views for Runs~2--11 reuse the same retained births, without connecting different runs into a shared trajectory. Their axes use each run's declared $L$ and key, rather than the pooled overview scale. These additional views add no observations or evidence of physical convergence.

\subsection{Recorded Ancestry Enables a Later-Assessment Disagreement to Be Inspected}
The historical Run~1 retains $1984$ births, complete recorded parent links, $2176$ island-slot memberships and $12$ migration events. The genealogy exposes reuse of ancestors rather than a simple branching tree. Its saved FEM-status categories distinguish $1455$ complete records from $529$ not evaluated; the latter are not failed solves.

Individual~1095 has the highest stored historical birth score ($f=1.15727$, generation~9). Its later strict-assessment summary records \code{cert\_pass\_all=false} and \code{seq\_ok=false}, although its strict lateral predicate is true. This is a documented disagreement between stored search preference and a later assessment criterion. It does not demonstrate a pair of candidates reversing rank or establish a general rank-transfer failure rate.

These are extrema of retained observations under their native preference directions, not newly evaluated designs. The native maximum has seven ties at serialized precision; its earliest original ID and existing public alias are reported without promoting a qualified champion. Contextual native fitness, screening deficits, weighted reporting summaries and later assessment scores have different producer and observation-stage bindings. Their values do not establish a single comparable global best.

The intermediate campaign retains $3649$ proposals in 57 birth files across 24 run directories. One-based candidate indexing joins $2259$ screens; $1390$ screen observations are missing. A separate retained weighted-reporting subset has six curve files and fifteen generation summaries. In Run~31, the archive score changes from $74.1675$ (Individual~2009) to $74.2073$ (Individual~2002), then stays at that value. An archive maximum is monotone by construction. The curve alone does not show continuing improvement in physical quality, and its unestablished producer/stage comparability precludes treating it as a controlled convergence experiment.

The automatic leaderboard separately retains one assessed export, Individual~2000, with zero qualified and no promoted champion. The historical $23\times18$ battery includes shared controls, blocked bindings and completeness flags; it is not a set of 414 independent mechanical tests. Individual~2002 is a reporting record with absent retained CAD, rather than an additional assessed export. Its vector is not transferred to the geometry or response fields of Individual~2000.

\subsection{Selected Solver Agreement and Refused Plate Extrapolation Support Different Conclusions}
On the same saved solid mesh, the scikit-fem/PyAMG and DOLFINx/PETSc paths agree within $0.05\%$ for the two retained recovered safety-factor observables. Separately, the plate mesh sequence does not admit its selected extrapolated error estimates: one observed order is nonpositive and the other sequence oscillates. Section~\ref{sec:verification} gives the inputs, recovery definitions and refusal reasons. Close shared-input agreement does not repair the extrapolation assumptions or establish physical qualification.
