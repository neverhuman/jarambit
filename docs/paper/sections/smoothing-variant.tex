\subsection{Geometric Smoothing of the Best Evolved Design}
\label{sec:smoothing}
We applied feature-preserving geometric smoothing to the tessellated mesh of the best evolved design by \code{W_ULTRALIGHT_v1}, \code{w406t3_r01_p10_c16} ($F=80.299390$, 14/18 battery, not qualified), and re-scored the smoothed geometry. Smoothing changes a tessellated mesh, not the STEP/B-rep CAD model.

\textit{Choice of design.} $F$ is recomputed with the canonical scorer (\code{ops/ci/ultralight_weighted_fitness.py}, which agrees with the Rust scorer in \code{auto-evolve}) from each retained Stage-2 scorecard and RS-v2 receipt of every run and wave since 2026-10-17 (Figure~\ref{fig:all-runs}). Among the 967 comparable assessments this design ranks first; it comes from the 2026-10-17/18 waves and carries two-sided isogrid pockets, lattice, relief and pierced-fuller windows. Its retained score was produced by an evaluator older than \code{W_ULTRALIGHT_v1}, so we re-assessed its genome with the current Stage-2 evaluator: the result, $80.299390$, is identical, as it is for two other designs of those waves. The 2026-10-16 evaluators are different: a stored $92.29$ re-assesses at $79.166355$, and the six such genomes re-assessed so far score $79.17$--$80.25$, so their 142 retained scorecards are excluded and the other genomes of those waves remain unranked. This design supersedes earlier versions of this variant: \code{ec1i005_r01_p14_c32} ($76.141571$), the best of the runs after 2026-10-18, which ranks 107th among distinct comparable genomes, and \code{ef3i17_r05_p14_c06} ($75.983539$). A still earlier version smoothed \code{ef2i91_r03_p14_c26}, whose run leaderboard reported 80.253391 but whose canonical score is 75.213491; those 2026-10-18 leaderboards overstate the canonical score by up to about 5 points and are not used. All inputs below are the re-assessment bundle of 2026-10-22.

\textit{Method.} The input STL was welded by exact coordinate equality into 44,980 vertices and 89,972 triangles. It is closed, edge-manifold, consistently oriented and connected, with Euler characteristic $-6$. We used the same pre-declared setting as the earlier runs (level~L4 of the smoothing ladder, \code{docs/paper/smoothing-ladder.pdf}): Taubin $\lambda/\mu$ filtering with $\lambda=0.5$ and $\mu=-0.5$ for 20 iterations, keeping only the component of each umbrella vector along the area-weighted unit vertex normal. Every vertex touching one of the 9,985 edges whose two face normals differ by more than $30^\circ$ was pinned (8,802 vertices), and total displacement was capped at 2\,mm. The tool rejects any iteration that reverses a face relative to the input; all 20 iterations completed.

\textit{Re-scoring.} Three of the twenty objectives depend on the exported 3D geometry, and those were re-measured. (1) Mass: the exporter computes 3D mass as enclosed volume times $4.43\,\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$. Smoothing changed the enclosed volume by $-17.14\,\mathrm{mm^3}$, giving $83.1472\,\mathrm{g}$ against $83.2231\,\mathrm{g}$. (2) Solid tip: the evaluator builds its P2 TET10 tip submodel (30\,mm cut, four layers) from the exported upper skin. We rebuilt it from the smoothed skin and repeated the \code{scikit-fem} solve and the independent DOLFINx~0.9.0 solve on the same saved mesh (with the pinned solver versions of the re-assessment that supplies the as-exported values, $0.6823$ and $0.7569$). (3) CAD/B-rep: this 5-point objective requires a valid single-solid STEP whose finger-ring bore matches the analytic torus. Smoothing outputs a triangle mesh and moved vertices by up to 0.214\,mm, so the objective is scored 0. The other seventeen objectives (plate-model responses, sharpness, point profile, ring, holder and aesthetics) are computed from the genome and carry over unchanged. Table~\ref{tab:smoothing-score} gives the result.

\textit{Results.} Smoothing moved 34,762 of 44,980 vertices, 6,338 of them by more than 0.01\,mm. The largest displacement was 0.214\,mm and the RMS displacement 0.0156\,mm. The enclosed volume changed by $-0.091\%$, and the bounding box changed only in $z$, by about 0.009\,mm on each side. The independent solid-tip safety factor at $1.82\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ rose from 0.6823 to 0.6905 at its nodal maximum and fell from 0.7569 to 0.7297 on the nodal average; the two solvers agree within $0.08\%$ on the smoothed mesh. Re-scored, $F$ falls from 80.299390 to 75.343635: $-5$ from the withheld CAD/B-rep objective, $+0.0246$ from the solid tip and $+0.0196$ from mass. Without the withheld objective, smoothing would change $F$ by $+0.044245$; the ten-level smoothing ladder (\code{docs/paper/smoothing-ladder.pdf}) shows that stronger smoothing lowers it. Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing-winner} shows the before and after appearance with the same camera and render settings, and the per-face displacement. The heatmap uses the mean of each face's three corner displacements on a linear scale from 0 to 0.214\,mm; its colours are unlit, so they encode the displacement value.

\textit{Limits.} The smoothed geometry is a triangle mesh. Smoothing does not reconstruct analytic surfaces, establish a continuous Hausdorff bound, test self-intersection, or preserve features beyond the pinned edges. The re-score re-measures only the three objectives that read the exported 3D geometry; the genome-derived objectives were not re-solved on the smoothed shape, whose largest displacement is 0.214\,mm. Neither score is a physical test.
