\section{From a 129-Column Genome Record to an Identifiable CAD}
\label{sec:genome}

\subsection{A Gene Is a Contract, Not Merely a Number}
A gene requires an explicit coordinate index, physical or discrete type, unit, box domain, coupling law, and decoder revision. A serialized floating-point token alone cannot distinguish a millimeter normal displacement from an angular bevel setting, a discrete feature-count selector, or a binary structural-layer switch. Those semantics determine whether a decoded B-rep or finite-element mesh is reproducible across software revisions.

Jarambit's native crescent representation encodes 128 continuous numerical coordinates paired with one categorical alloy state, and decodes them into a planform and 2.5D thickness field. In every evaluated population table across Cohort~3 (\text{Run~2}--\text{Run~11}, $1{,}954{,}560$ evaluations across 10 retained runs of 801 generations) and Cohort~1 (\text{Run~1}, $1{,}984$ historical births), each row contains 146 columns: 17 scalar evaluation and search-metadata columns (columns $0..16$), followed by 128 numerical \code{f64} gene coordinates (columns $17..144$) and one categorical alloy column (\code{material}, column $145$, taking values \code{Ti-6Al-4V} or \code{Ti-3Al-2.5V}), yielding 129 genome-and-material fields per birth record.

\subsection{The Four Structural Blocks of the 128 Numeric Genes}
The 128 numeric coordinates partition into four functional blocks that reflect the structural hierarchy of the decoder:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Block A: Silhouette Normal Control Points (\code{cp00}--\code{cp39}, indices $0..39$, 40 coordinates).} Each coordinate specifies a normal displacement in millimeters from the 40-station reference outline. Station-specific bounds restrict displacements to $\pm 1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ on the outer ring arc ($i\in[0,4]\cup[36,39]$), $\pm 2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ along the tip approach ($i\in[15,22]$), and $\pm 3.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ along the belly, spine, and handle arches.
\item \textbf{Block B: Planform, Stock, Bevel, Relief, Lattice, Fuller, and Edge Coordinates (indices $40..89$, 50 coordinates).} Indices $40..44$ parameterize global planform and ring dimensions (blade drop angle, grip offset $\Delta x_{\text{grip}}$, ring wall thickness, ring inner diameter, and neck fillet radius); indices $45..49$ define stock thickness $t_{\text{stock}}$ ($[4.5,6.0]\,\mathrm{mm}$), tip thickness $t_{\text{tip}}$ ($[2.2,5.0]\,\mathrm{mm}$), taper origin $x_{0,\text{taper}}$, and inside/spine bevel angles; indices $50..89$ parameterize the comet groove, ergonomics, three-tier serration depths, handle relief pocket (depth, width, and exponent), dimple lattice, blade fuller, handle flute, surface texture mode, cord wrap, grip pad, and cutting-edge thickness ($[0.25,1.20]\,\mathrm{mm}$).
\item \textbf{Block C: Internal-Machining Coordinates (indices $90..116$, 27 coordinates).} These 27 coordinates control triangular isogrid pocketing (depth, pitch, grading, handle start, and land width), pierced skeletonization windows (window count through decay rate), ring lightening flutes (depth, width, and arc span), and angled micro-perforation slots (depth through handle start).
\item \textbf{Block D: Continuous-Rail Layer (indices $117..127$, 11 coordinates).} The final 11 numeric coordinates encode the binary continuous-rail layer switch $p_{1,\text{on}}\in\{0,1\}$, continuous perimeter rail width $w_{\text{rail}}$ ($[2.2,5.5]\,\mathrm{mm}$), solid neck length $l_{\text{neck}}$ ($[6.0,24.0]\,\mathrm{mm}$), interior pocket depth and corner radius ($d_{\text{pocket}}, r_{\text{pocket}}$), active spline-knot count $k_n\in\{3,4,5\}$, and five interior thickness knots \code{k0}--\code{k4} ($[1.5,6.0]\,\mathrm{mm}$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Decoder Coupling, Clamping, and Inactive Coordinates}
These 129 fields are not independent degrees of freedom. Before phenotype construction, the decoder clamp enforces box bounds on all 128 numeric coordinates together with deterministic cross-gene coupling laws:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  t_{\text{tip}} &\le t_{\text{stock}}, & x_{1,\text{comet}} &\ge x_{0,\text{comet}} + 15\,\mathrm{mm}, \\
  s_{1,\text{fuller}} &\ge s_{0,\text{fuller}} + 0.12, & s_{1,\text{win}} &\ge s_{0,\text{win}} + 0.12,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
while rounding discrete coordinates (surface texture mode $\in\{0,1,2\}$, window count $\in\{0,\dots,8\}$, continuous-rail switch $p_{1,\text{on}}\in\{0,1\}$, and spline-knot count $k_n\in\{3,4,5\}$). When $p_{1,\text{on}} < 0.5$, the continuous-rail thickness law is inactive in the decoder even though its 11 numeric coordinates remain serialized in the genome record; conversely, when $p_{1,\text{on}} \ge 0.5$, the continuous perimeter rail and spline-knot thickness law supersede legacy interior pocket features. Preserving all 129 columns losslessly is therefore essential to distinguish an inactive coordinate mutation from a geometric change.

\subsection{Candidate, Genome, and Export Identity}
Because multiple birth events can produce identical clamped coordinates (for example, Cohort~1 \text{Run~1} records $1{,}984$ births across $1{,}965$ distinct gene serializations), Jarambit separates birth/individual identity (\text{Individual~ID}), genome serialization identity, population-slot appearance, and exported CAD/assessment bundle identity. When comparing parent and child rows across the 128 numeric CSV coordinates (columns $17..144$, \code{cp00}--\code{k4}), changed-coordinate counts are defined by exact floating-point inequality ($\Delta \neq 0$) on the stored CSV tokens, and raw-coordinate Euclidean $L_2$ norms across those 128 columns mix heterogeneous units (millimeters, degrees, dimensionless ratios, and discrete selectors) rather than measuring physical geometric distance. For Individual~2000, the STEP solid ($1$ solid, $1$ shell, $55{,}698$ face tokens), the render mesh ($44{,}994$ vertices, $90{,}000$ triangles), the $0.25\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ plate mesh ($13{,}503$ nodes, $25{,}209$ triangles), and the $P_2$ tetrahedral solid tip submodel ($91{,}008$ tetrahedra, $416{,}583$ displacement degrees of freedom) represent distinct discretizations with separate provenance bindings documented in Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}.
