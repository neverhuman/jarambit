\section{Conclusion: From Unexpected Geometry to Valuable Products}
The future of computational engineering is the ability to formulate and explore geometric families, explain their tradeoffs, and connect every conclusion to verifiable evidence. Jarambit's engineering depth becomes legible when 16 diagnostic predicates, ten overlapping requirement groups, 18 heterogeneous evaluator columns, and 178 assurance definitions are explained through their physical and numerical roles rather than advertised as one aggregate test count.

The retained evidence supports a bounded conclusion: the highest historical search rank failed later strict assessment, and the separately assessed automatic export reports $<0.05\%$ matched solid-solver agreement while its three-level plate GCI estimates are refused. Reconstructing the three-cohort population archive and historical battery within their declared scopes establishes neither a qualified product, global optimality, agent-discovered representations, unprecedented manufacturing tolerance, nor physical reliability.

Joining representation research with additive manufacturing and metrology opens prospective opportunities across aerospace fixtures, robotics, thermal management, medical-device research, scientific instruments, and energy systems, each requiring domain-specific models, physical measurements, and qualification.
